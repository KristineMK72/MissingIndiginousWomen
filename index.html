Tina Fontaine (Winnipeg, MB, 2014)

Age: 15 | Status: Homicide | Location: Red River, Winnipeg.

An Anishinaabe girl from Sagkeeng First Nation, Tina was failed by child welfare systems. Last seen leaving a shelter, her body was found wrapped in plastic after days in the river. Her death ignited national outrage, leading to "Drag the Red" searches and calls for MMIWG inquiry. The accused was acquitted, highlighting justice gaps. Family: "She was a bubbly girl who loved to dance."

Source: National Inquiry Report.

Helen Claire Frost (Prince George, BC, 1970)

Age: 17 | Status: Missing | Location: Prince George, Highway of Tears.

Indigenous teen from the Lake Babine Nation, Helen left home on October 20, 1970, to visit friends. Family reported her missing the next day, but police dismissed it as a runaway. No leads since; her case is one of the earliest on the Highway of Tears, symbolizing decades of neglect. Family continues annual vigils.

Recent: Part of RCMP's 18 acknowledged cases.

Jean Virginia Sampare (Gitsegukla, BC, 1971)

Age: 18 | Status: Missing | Location: Gitsegukla, near Highway 16.

From the Gitxsan Nation, Jean vanished on October 14, 1971, while chatting with her cousin by the Skeena River. She went to retrieve a jacket and never returned. Police speculated suicide or runaway, but family insists foul play. Her case spurred early advocacy; remains unfound.

Impact: Inspired the Highway of Tears Symposium in 2006.

Monica Ignas (Thornhill, BC, 1974)

Age: 14 | Status: Homicide | Location: Thornhill, near Terrace.

Tsilhqot'in Nation girl, Monica disappeared December 13, 1974, after school. Her strangled body was found in April 1975 in a forested area off Highway 16. Witnesses reported a suspicious car, but investigation stalled. Unsolved; family faced racism in police response.

Update: Featured in ongoing E-Pana project for serial cases.

Ramona Lisa Wilson (Smithers, BC, 1994)

Age: 16 | Status: Homicide | Location: Smithers, Highway 16.

Wet'suwet'en Nation teen hitchhiking to a festival on June 8, 1994. Remains found April 1995 in a forested ditch. Unsolved despite DNA; family hosts annual memorial walks. Ramona dreamed of becoming a hairdresser.

Advocacy: Her story in "Finding Ramona" documentary.

Delphine Anne Camelia Nikal (Smithers, BC, 1990)

Age: 15 | Status: Missing (Remains found 2022) | Location: Smithers.

From the Tsay Keh Dene Nation, Delphine hitchhiked home from a party on June 29, 1990. Human remains linked to her were found in 2022 near Prince George, but cause of death undetermined. Cousin of Cecilia Nikal (also missing). Case highlights jurisdictional issues.

Recent: RCMP confirmation in 2022; family seeks full closure.

Alberta Gail Williams (Prince Rupert, BC, 1989)

Age: 24 | Status: Homicide | Location: Prince Rupert, east of city.

Tsimshian Nation mother of two, last seen August 25, 1989, after a night out. Strangled and sexually assaulted body found days later off Highway 16. Unsolved; part of E-Pana serial killer investigation. Family: "She was loving and full of life."

Update: DNA evidence pursued in 2020s.

Tamara Lynn Chipman (Prince George, BC, 1998)

Age: 22 | Status: Missing | Location: Prince George.

Wet'suwet'en and Cree woman, Tamara vanished September 21, 1998, while hitchhiking. Struggled with addiction; last seen entering a truck. Unsolved; her son advocates today. Case exemplifies urban-rural vulnerabilities on the Highway.

Recent: Featured in 2025 billboards by Carrier Sekani Family Services.

Aielah Saric Auger (Prince George, BC, 2008)

Age: 15 | Status: Homicide | Location: Near Prince George.

St'át'imc Nation teen, Aielah went missing February 14, 2008. Remains found October 2008 in woods off Highway 97 (linked to Tears). Cause: undetermined homicide. Police criticized for slow response; family pushed for inquiry.

Impact: Sparked BC's MMIWG action plan.

Initiatives & Updates (BC Focus)

In 2025, families and Carrier Sekani Family Services unveiled four new billboards honoring victims along Highway 16. BC invested $75M in cell coverage expansion (550+ km by 2027) to aid safety. Tears to Hope Society provides education; 33 recommendations from 2006 Symposium remain key. Over 40 cases estimated; RCMP lists 18 unsolved.

The Silent Crisis: Focus on British Columbia's Highway of Tears

The Highway of Tears, a 719-km stretch of Highway 16 from Prince George to Prince Rupert, has seen at least 18 confirmed cases of missing and murdered Indigenous women since 1969 (RCMP), with families estimating over 40. Rooted in colonialism, inadequate policing, and isolation, this epidemic reflects broader MMIWG genocide. Recent 2025 updates include new memorials and infrastructure investments, but justice lags.

Symbol: Red dresses hang along the route in remembrance.
